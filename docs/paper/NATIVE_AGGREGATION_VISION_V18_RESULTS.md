# V18: native semantic gating passes the MMReD Vision extrapolation milestone

V18 passes the preregistered primary and practical gates in both fixed seeds. Training used sequences of at most 16 frames; on fresh canonical MMReD Vision contexts, semantic gating achieves **132/136 at N32 (97.06%) and 131/136 at N64 (96.32%)** in each seed. The matched all-open models score 0/136 at both lengths. The [independent report 442913](../../outputs/native_aggregation_vlm/v18/report_442913/analysis.json) verifies complete native answers, every retained trajectory, final parameter identities, data provenance and campaign accounting.

The method uses the frozen native vocabulary head at each local image’s original question position. It computes `g=max(0,p(1)−p(0))` from full-vocabulary probabilities, then multiplies every coordinate of a learned 96-dimensional tanh payload by that gate before SUM aggregation. A learned readout adds a residual to the global native state. Gates remain fixed during answer generation. There is no reference bank, null predictor, external arithmetic tally or generated reasoning trace.

The two arms share the 1,041,600-parameter architecture, initialization within each seed, training data/order, CE plus residual-consistency loss, and native probe work. Each fit ends at the fixed 4,590th update. All four models achieve 64/64 on their single descriptive development sweep. Thus the controls’ zero test accuracy is an out-of-distribution collapse after successful in-range performance, rather than evidence of optimizer failure. The four final parameter hashes are distinct; matching gated accuracies do not represent a reused checkpoint.

These use 10,000 K-stratified family draws, seed 20261118, shared across lengths and fitted models. They describe example uncertainty conditional on these two seeds, not population uncertainty over training seeds. The [verified figures 442918](../../outputs/native_aggregation_vlm/v18/plots/plot_442918/native_vision_v18_accuracy.pdf) show the same counts without rescoring.

Every gated answer is a valid integer terminated by native EOS; all first numeral tokens are correct. Each gated model nevertheless makes nine complete-answer errors: four at N32 and five at N64. These are undercounts 13→12,14→13 or 16→15, concentrated in the second numeral token. K16 remains 5/8 at each length. First-token accuracy therefore overstates complete-answer performance.

This algebraic decomposition retains Step rerendering and FP32 multiplication residuals. It does not prove that gate variation causes the remaining answer errors.

The V18 campaign through this report costs **3,681 allocated GPU-seconds**, with at most four concurrent GPUs and all failed/zero allocations retained. Inference processes N local streams plus one global stream. For T generated tokens, it uses T batched model calls, one vision prefill, T ordinary native norm/head calls, and one additional full-batch norm/head probe at the origin. Both arms pay that probe cost; batching does not make N+1 streams free.

This is a counting extrapolation result under the canonical data law. Complete contexts are fresh against 30 prior manifests, but visual atoms may recur and every answer value 0–16 appeared during training. The result does not establish that vector payloads outperform a scalar sufficient statistic, that aggregation bandwidth transfers across tasks, or that the benefit composes with reasoning. Those claims require separate controls and experiments.

A subsequent exploratory [payload analysis 442933](../../outputs/native_aggregation_vlm/v18/payload_rank/run_442933/analysis.json) makes the limitation more concrete. At the original query, the largest singular direction accounts for more than0.99999999992 of positive-payload energy in both gated models; within-question variation accounts for about6×10⁻¹². A question-specific empirical vector times total gate mass approximates the positive aggregate with mean relative error below10⁻⁶ atN64. These means were estimated from the test sample and never deployed. This supports a weighted-count interpretation, while leaving causal use of tiny components untested. V18 should therefore support a counting result, not a claim of richer vector aggregation. The CPU analysis used5seconds and no model or head calls.
