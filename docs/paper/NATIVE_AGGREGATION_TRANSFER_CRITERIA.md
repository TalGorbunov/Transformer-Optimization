# Native aggregation: evidence required for transfer

This memo separates completed evidence from prospective experiments. It was prepared without inspecting V8 outcomes. The answer-value holdout and reasoning comparison below are **proposals, not registered or released experiments**.

The objective is a learned aggregation operation that produces native language-model outputs and can be reused at every generated query. In the parallel implementation, each image has a complete local prompt; generated global tokens become a shared prefix for subsequent local and global computation. Learned fusion writes into the global state before the native final norm and vocabulary head. No external tally supplies the answer. One batched invocation still executes multiple local streams and maintains their caches; it does not mean the computation of one ordinary joint prompt.

The claim ladder is: correct native execution; useful generalization to longer scenes; transfer to answer values absent from adaptation; benefit during free reasoning; and transfer beyond this rendered world. Passing one level does not establish the next. In particular, a software cache/replay check establishes implementation behavior, not counting accuracy or reasoning benefit.

V7 established a useful but limited architecture contrast. With training at N8/N16 and K0–8, the parallel model scored 108/108 at N16 in both seeds and 106/108 and 90/108 at N32. At N64 it scored only 16/108 and 0/108; all four models scored 0/128 on unseen K9–16. Its relative criterion passed against the joint-state control, but the practical criterion failed. Both arms had the same 1,041,600 trainable parameters; their inference computation was not matched. The experiment therefore supports the complete independent-local-computation-plus-fusion package, not an isolated SUM effect or increased information capacity. [Completed V7 results](NATIVE_AGGREGATION_VISION_V7_RESULTS.md)

The architectural ingredients have close precedents. FiD independently encodes question–passage inputs and fuses their representations in a generative decoder. HiLS learns chunk retrieval under language-model loss and combines query-conditioned chunk reads. Neither independent evidence processing nor hierarchical native fusion should carry a novelty claim. [FiD, section 3](https://aclanthology.org/2021.eacl-main.74.pdf); [HiLS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02980).

V8 tests a narrower hypothesis: requiring equal-answer N8/N16 scenes with the same question to produce consistent native residuals reduces length-dependent nuisance. CE and consistency conditions use identical architecture, pairing, initialization, presentation budgets, and inference. The auxiliary penalty compares FP32 residuals separately at count and EOS positions, normalized by the fixed frozen global-state norm. A successful matched contrast would support this training intervention without added inference work.

Its strongest alternative explanation is a stable residual prototype for each trained count. Same-answer alignment can suppress length variation while learning only familiar answer classes. Cross-domain same-class attraction already appears in CCSA, alongside classification and separation losses; SizeShiftReg already uses training-only size transformations to improve size generalization. Our residual placement and native evaluation are the empirical question, not a new consistency principle. [CCSA](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_iccv_2017/html/Motiian_Unified_Deep_Supervised_ICCV_2017_paper.html); [SizeShiftReg](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07888).

The proposed decisive follow-up holds out answer values: train on K={0,2,4,6,8}, reserving K={1,3,5,7} from both adaptation and development selection. Reuse only permitted scene/prefix entries from the frozen cache. Deterministically reweight retained pairs to preserve 77,760 scene presentations and 4,860 updates, identically for CE and consistency. This requires no new backbone-feature harvest. Fresh paired N16/N32/N64 tests can retain 452 evaluations: 72 odd-count anchors, 36 seen-even anchors, and 128 separate K9–16 examples. Preregister exact odd-count performance at N64 as the decisive endpoint, including an absolute usefulness threshold and the matched CE contrast. Improvements confined to seen counts fail the transfer claim. Equal transfer by both conditions supports the architecture, not the extra loss.

Score complete native numerals plus EOS, with malformed and truncated outputs incorrect. Report K9 separately from multi-digit K10–16; predicting their first digit is insufficient. These are unseen adaptation answers, not new vocabulary entries: the pretrained head can emit them, although training may suppress their probabilities. Do not rescue failure through an external decoder, rounding, or test-fitted calibration. Successful held-out-value prediction would still not establish transfer to unseen visual primitives or real-world scenes.

The minimal reasoning experiment crosses the validated treatment with output policy within one reasoning backbone:

| Same backbone and scenes | Direct answer | Native free reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Matched CE control | C-direct | C-reason |
| Consistency treatment | T-direct | T-reason |

Use fresh paired scenes, fixed prompts and budgets, identical compute limits within each column, and no supplied scratchpads. Establish long-cache execution first; retain complete trajectories, strict final-answer grammar, EOS, truncations, latency, and memory. Benefits in both T-direct versus C-direct and T-reason versus C-reason demonstrate persistence under reasoning. T-reason improving over T-direct additionally supports composition; a positive interaction is not required. This is a policy comparison, not proof that token count alone causes improvement. Artificial two-count prefixes do not substitute for it, and comparing Qwen with Cosmos cannot isolate reasoning from different training histories.
