## Novelty threats (ranked)

**1. Barbero et al. 2024, "Transformers need glasses!" (arXiv:2406.04267, NeurIPS 2024).**
The direct precedent for "over-squashing, imported from GNNs, happens in decoder-only LLMs and
breaks counting." What it does: proves last-token representational collapse and token-sensitivity
loss in *text-only* decoder LLMs on counting/copying, and mitigates with prompt-level token
insertion. What it does NOT do: no vision-language models, no multi-frame/multi-image evidence
aggregation, no mechanistic localization of a carrier pathway, no learned virtual-node/carrier
tokens, no trained adapter on a frozen model, and no demonstration that a *readout* (as opposed to
mixing) stage is the residual bottleneck. **Safe wording:** claim novelty for "identifying and
mechanistically localizing the aggregation bottleneck in a *frozen vision-language* transformer on
a multi-frame counting task, and *relieving* it with GNN-derived interventions (rewiring masks,
learned virtual-node carrier tokens, injective sum adapters)" — cite Barbero as establishing the
phenomenon in text LLMs, and position the thesis as its VLM instantiation + remedy. Never phrase the
contribution as "we show over-squashing exists in transformers" — that claim is theirs (and Lee
arXiv:2512.09182 now owns the general GNN-lens framing too).

**2. Wen et al. 2024, "Victor" — visual register tokens in VLMs (arXiv:2410.14072).**
Closest architectural overlap: learnable register tokens appended inside a VLM's language tower,
trained alongside a projector while the backbone towers stay frozen. But its registers exist to
*compress* visual tokens for **efficiency** (drop visual tokens after layer k; accepts an accuracy
loss), not to add aggregation capacity, and it never evaluates counting/aggregation or connects to
over-squashing. **Safe wording:** "unlike register-token compression schemes (Victor; Darcet et al.),
our carrier tokens are virtual nodes in the GNN sense — added to *increase* aggregation capacity on
an identified bottleneck, with the original tokens retained — and are validated causally on the
aggregation task." Cite it; do not let a reviewer find it first.

**3. Blayney et al. 2025, "gLSTM" (arXiv:2510.08450) — name + framing collision.**
Same name as the thesis's adapter family, same over-squashing vocabulary, and a capacity-based
theory of the phenomenon — but for message-passing GNNs on graphs, not frozen VLMs. Threat is
citation-hygiene: a reader will assume the thesis's "gLSTM adapter" is this paper's method.
**Safe wording:** cite it explicitly, state the name collision in a footnote (or rename the adapter
in the thesis, e.g. "memory-carrier adapter"), and note the complementary claim: they add storage
capacity *inside the GNN being trained*; the thesis adds capacity *outside a frozen transformer*
via adapter-injected virtual-node state.

Honorable mentions (cite, lower risk): Lee arXiv:2512.09182 (owns the general "transformer failures
through the GNN lens" survey framing — cite to preempt); Darcet et al. ICLR 2024 (registers, but
trained-in during pretraining and aimed at feature-map artifacts, not aggregation on a frozen
model); Barbero et al. 2025 attention-sinks (supports, doesn't compete).
