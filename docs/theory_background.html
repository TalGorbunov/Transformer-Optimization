1The object of study: attention messages

Everything in the theory is a statement about one measurable vector quantity inside the frozen model's forward pass.

At layer L, the carrier token c (the queried "Park" token) attends over the whole context. Attention output is exactly a weighted sum of value vectors, so it splits exactly by frame:

msgf→c = o_proj( Σj ∈ frame f A[c,j] · vj )

A[c,j] is the attention weight from carrier to source token j, vj its value vector, and o_proj is the attention block's output projection — the linear matrix WO that maps concatenated head outputs back into residual-stream coordinates. Because o_proj is linear, the per-frame split survives it: the carrier's total attention update is exactly Σf msgf. No probing, no approximation — arithmetic on the forward pass.

Decomposition

Two clouds first — then one equation

Split the frames by their ground-truth label and each message is just its class mean plus scatter:

evidence frame:  mk = μ + δ + εk   ·   non-evidence frame:  mk = μ + εk

Writing the label as a bit ek folds both cases into one line — the form we keep, because summing over frames turns Σek into the count (§3):

mk = μ + ek·δ + εk
  • μ — the baseline: the mean message of the non-evidence frames — what every frame writes just for being a frame of this scene. μ = mean{ mk : ek = 0 }
  • δ — the signal: the difference of the two class means — the one direction that says "the character was there." δ = mean{ mk : ek = 1 } − mean{ mk : ek = 0 }
  • ek ∈ {0,1} — the frame's ground-truth label (was the character in the room?) — given by the dataset, never estimated.
  • εk — the noise: the residual — everything left after removing the class mean (layout, other characters, poses). εk = mk − μ − ek·δ

μ and δ are plain class averages and ε the leftover — nothing is fitted. The only modeling assumption is ε's distribution (Gaussian, same covariance in both classes) — a hypothesis about the data, not a fact, and §5 tests it per dataset: it passes on MMRED (image, text, and natural-image variants) and fails informatively on real video, where the theory retreats to ordering claims.

measured  ‖μ‖ ≈ 87 · ‖δ‖ ≈ 1 (≈1% of μ) · noise spread along the naive axis σ ≈ 6 — the signal is 1% of the message and ⅙ of the noise.

2Reading the clouds — from ruler to d′

Every linear readout — a probe, a next-layer weight, anything — is an axis: it turns each message into one number. So "how separable are the clouds?" really asks "how separated are the two histograms on the best axis?" — and answering that needs the right notion of distance. The obvious one lies.

Step 1 · the object

A readout is an axis — and every axis gets one number

Pick a direction w and project: every message m becomes the single number wᵀm. Now project §1's decomposition term by term — and the whole 3584-dimensional model collapses into a one-dimensional one:

wᵀm = wᵀμ + e·(wᵀδ) + wᵀε

On the axis, the two classes are two histograms with two centers and a shared width (E[· | class] = the average over that class's frames):

a = E[wᵀm | non-evid] = wᵀμ   ·   b = E[wᵀm | evid] = wᵀ(μ+δ)   ·   σw = std(wᵀε) = √( wᵀ Σ w )
d′(w) = (b − a) / σw = wᵀδ / √( wᵀ Σ w )

Here Σ is the covariance of the noise ε — the matrix recording the noise cloud's shape (Σ = mean over frames of εkεkᵀ: diagonal = each direction's noise variance, off-diagonal = how directions co-vary), and its one job is the identity wᵀΣw = Var(wᵀε) — the noise variance you inherit by reading along w. So d′(w) is the bump-center gap counted in bump widths: the z-score idea — never count a gap in raw units, count it in noise units along the direction you are reading. Threshold at the midpoint of a and b and the balanced accuracy is Φ(d′(w)/2). The whole section is now one question: which axis maximizes this ratio?

Step 2 · the derivation

The best axis, in four annotated lines

The obvious axis — δ̂ = δ/‖δ‖, point from one cloud center at the other — is measurably bad: held out at the deployed room-token carrier it reads d′(δ̂) = 1.12 where the best axis reads 2.47 from the same vectors (0.33 vs 3.35 in the question-first frame-reps regime). The reason: the noise is anisotropic — loud in some directions, nearly silent in others — and loudest almost exactly where δ̂ points. A big gap in raw units is worthless if the noise there is bigger. So maximize properly:

maximize over w:  d′(w) = (b−a)/σw = |wᵀδ| / √( wᵀ Σ w )   — scale-free: only w's direction matters
substitute u = Σ1/2w:  d′ = | u · (Σ−1/2δ) | / |u|   — the denominator collapses: this substitution IS whitening
Cauchy–Schwarz:  maximum when u ∝ Σ−1/2δ   — a dot product is largest when the vectors are parallel
map back:  w* = Σ⁻¹δ,  max value = √( δᵀ Σ⁻¹ δ )   — the Mahalanobis distance between the class means ≡ d′

Read the middle step slowly — it is the whole idea. In u-coordinates (u = Σ1/2w, equivalently data z = Σ−1/2m) the noise cloud is a unit sphere: no direction is louder than any other, so the best axis is trivially "point at the other cloud's mean." The formula w* = Σ⁻¹δ is that trivial answer translated back into the original, tilted coordinates. And the maximum value earns Mahalanobis (1936) its name: the gap between the class means counted in noise-standard-deviations — in one dimension it is literally the z-score |a−b|/σ.

Why Σ⁻¹ instead of pointing at the gap: a component of δ lying along a loud noise direction buys numerator but costs more denominator — a bad trade. Σ⁻¹ reweights every direction by 1/variance, so w* leans toward directions where the gap is cheap in noise. That is why w* can be nearly orthogonal to δ̂ (measured cos ≈ 0.03 at the deployed carrier): almost all of δ's raw length lies in loud directions, and w* abandons it to harvest the small clean component.

sanity checks  spherical noise (Σ = σ²·I) → w* ∝ δ̂, d′ = ‖δ‖/σ · one dimension → |a−b|/σ, the z-score · d′ from a balanced classifier: d′ = 2Φ⁻¹(accuracy).
Payoff

d′ — one number, and it is the Mahalanobis distance

d′ = max over axes of gap/width = √( δᵀ Σ⁻¹ δ ) = D_M(μ_E, μ_N)

Two definitions, one number: the best achievable histogram separation and the noise-unit distance between the class centers coincide — that is what the derivation proved. It resolves the paradox this project tripped on: the naive axis measured the gap in raw units along the loudest direction (0.33 in the frame-reps regime, 1.12 at the deployed carrier); a trained linear probe is free to whiten and reads the Mahalanobis value (3.35 / 2.47). Both numbers were always true — they answer different questions. d′ is a descriptive quantity, valid with or without Gaussian classes; only means and covariances enter it.

Do sklearn probes find w*? LDA (LinearDiscriminantAnalysis) estimates Σ⁻¹δ by construction (we use Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage for stability in 3584-d). Logistic regression and ridge converge to essentially the same direction for near-Gaussian classes given enough data. That is why any trained linear probe reads the whitened value where the raw mean-difference reads the naive one.

measured  frame reps: naive 0.33 → whitened 3.35 · deployed carrier: naive 1.12 → whitened 2.47, cos(δ̂,w*) 0.03 · text 2.45 (block) · isolated forwards 6.4–6.6.
d′ → accuracy

Balanced accuracy Φ(d′/2) — the first place the Gaussian shape earns its keep

Everything so far needed no distribution. But once E4 (§5) has checked that the two projected bumps really are equal-width Gaussians, d′ converts to an accuracy in closed form. Put the threshold at the midpoint fence between a and b — each center sits d′/2 noise-widths from it. A frame is misclassified exactly when its noise pushes it across the fence, i.e. past d′/2 in the wrong direction:

P(error) = 1 − Φ(d′/2)  (same for both classes)   ⇒   balanced accuracy = Φ(d′/2)  ·  inverse: d′ = 2Φ⁻¹(acc)

Under Theorem 1 (§3) this is not just the best threshold — it is the ceiling of any decision rule at this locus. The identity runs both ways: measure a held-out probe's balanced accuracy and you have measured d′; measure d′ and you have bounded every possible per-frame detector. (Per-frame only — what happens when N frames are summed is §4.) The project's own values, translated:

dictionary  d′ 0.51 (native axis) → 0.60 · 1.12 (naive, deployed) → 0.71 · 2.47 (w*, deployed) → 0.89 · 3.35 (frame reps) → 0.95 · 6.5 (isolated forwards) → 0.999.
One law, two axes

w* is what could be read; the native axis is what the model reads

w* is normative — the best possible reading direction, computed from the data geometry. The native axis is descriptive — the direction the frozen model's own readout effectively uses at the same locus, extracted from its behavior (E6, [2026-07-05]). Measured relationship: cos(native, w*) ≈ 0.005 — essentially orthogonal — carrying d′ 0.51 of the available 2.47. The same law prices both: plug w*'s d′ into 2Φ(d′/2√N)−1 and you predict what a probe can extract (0.375; measured 0.363); plug the native axis's d′ into the same formula and you predict what the model actually scores (0.17; measured 0.21–0.24). One equation, two directions, two correctly predicted accuracies — the model-vs-probe gap is not a mystery, it is a measured angle (Wall Ⅲ, §10; the full story: §9). Replicated on InternVL (native 0.19 → pred 0.135, meas 0.117–0.137) and text-MMRED (0.80 → 0.163, meas 0.165).

Boundary marker

What has NOT been assumed yet

Everything above used only means and covariances — no distribution was assumed anywhere: the formula d′(w), the derivation, w*, the Mahalanobis identity are all distribution-free. The shape of the clouds — Gaussian, equal covariance — enters only at the next step, when a measured d′ is converted into probabilities: the Φ(d′/2) accuracy, the optimality theorems, and the counting law of §3–§4. That shape is a hypothesis about the data, and §5 tests it per dataset before any closed-form number is quoted.

Refinement

ρ — cross-frame noise correlation

ρ = the average correlation between two different frames' noise projections in the same sample: ρ = corr(w·εi, w·εj), i ≠ j. If frames are processed independently, ρ ≈ 0 and summed noise grows as √N. If one forward pass lets frames contaminate each other, ρ > 0 and the summed noise variance becomes σ²·N·(1+(N−1)ρ) — correlated noise never averages away (ρ = 0.1 at N = 128 inflates variance ≈ 13.7×).

measured  joint pass ρ ≈ +0.09…+0.13 · isolated forwards ρ ≈ +0.01 — "single-pass superposition," quantified.

3Theorems the theory stands on

Four classical results; each stated in one breath, with what it buys us.

Theorem 1 · Fisher 1936

For two equal-covariance Gaussians, the best possible classifier is linear

The Bayes boundary is the decision surface of the rule that minimizes error: "answer the class with higher posterior probability." In general it can be any shape. For two Gaussians with the same Σ, the log-likelihood-ratio's quadratic terms cancel, leaving a linear function of m — so the optimal boundary is the hyperplane perpendicular to w*, and the best achievable accuracy of any rule, linear or not, is Φ(d′/2).

Unequal variances? The quadratic terms no longer cancel: the optimal rule becomes quadratic (QDA), a linear rule is no longer optimal, and Φ(d′/2) stops being the ceiling — a readout can exploit the quieter class. This is not a breakdown of the framework, only of the closed form; E4's std-ratio check (§5) tells you when you are in this regime (text-CWE is: its filler class is near-deterministic, std-ratio 1.2→10.8 with N, and the linear law duly under-predicts).

Theorem 2 · sufficiency

The matched filter w*ᵀS carries everything the sum knows about the count

Let S = Σk mk be the pooled sum with g evidence frames. Under the model, S ~ N(Nμ + g·δ, NΣ): the count g moves the mean of S only along δ. Writing out the Gaussian density and factoring (Fisher–Neyman), every g-dependent term touches S only through the single scalar t = w*ᵀS. So t is a sufficient statistic: any function of S — an MLP, anything — can be no more informative about g than the best function of t. Consequence: no post-sum nonlinearity can beat the matched filter.

measured  MLP-on-sum never beats linear, 21/21 regimes (Δ −0.04…−0.58).
Theorem 3 · the counting law

Exact-count accuracy of the best readout of the sum

P(exact | interior g) = 2Φ( d′ / 2√N ) − 1   (boundary counts one-sided; mix over the count prior)

Sketch: along w*, each unit of count moves t by ‖δ‖ while the summed noise has width σw√N — so consecutive counts are d′/√N apart in noise units, and nearest-integer rounding succeeds when the noise stays within half a step. Two measured inputs (d′, N), zero fitted parameters. This is the number every "law pred" column in §6 contains.

Theorem 4 · DeepSets (Zaheer 2017) + Wagstaff 2019

Where the nonlinearity must sit

Any permutation-invariant set function has the form ρ(Σk φ(mk)) — a per-element map φ before the sum, then any readout ρ after. A plain sum is φ = id and can only realize linearly-separable set functions. DeepSets was written for set-learning architectures, not transformers — it applies here because the carrier's aggregate is a sum over the frame-set, so the theorem dictates where fixing it is even possible: per-frame, pre-sum. Combined with Theorem 2 (post-sum is capped), the two theorems bracket the design space from both sides. Wagstaff adds: representing counts up to N needs latent width ≥ N (our d_mem knee at exactly 8).

Soft vs sharp gate. The working per-frame φ is a threshold σ(κ·(w·mk)). κ is the steepness: soft (small κ) outputs graded values like 0.4/0.7 and lets magnitude noise leak into the sum; sharp (large κ, ≈ a step) commits each frame to 0/1, converting noise into occasional bit-errors that partially cancel. Measured: κ = 0 (linear) 0.09 → soft 0.73 → sharp (κ=20) 1.00.

Extension · multiclass (rooms)

K-channel form for per-frame labels beyond binary

If the per-frame label has K values (which room, 1…6), run the same machinery per channel: ekr = 1[frame k shows room r], each with its own δr and d′r (measured 4.5–5.9 at L14). Pooled tallies tr = Σk er then feed the task's reduction. For rooms-visited the reduction is support size #{r : tr > 0} — provably nonlinear in the tallies, so no linear readout can express it: measured linear 0.40 < per-channel threshold 0.65 ≪ decode-then-count 0.99 on the same pooled vector. The binary theory is the K = 2 case, not a limitation of the framework.

4What summing does — signal adds like a ruler, noise like a drunk

§1 showed the carrier's aggregate is a sum over frames. Project it onto any reading axis and the failure mode falls out in three short lines.

the sum, projected

The score of the sum is the sum of scores

Pool the frames, S = Σk mk, with g evidence frames among N, and read with any axis w — projection is linear, so:

wᵀS = N(wᵀμ) + g·(wᵀδ) + Σk wᵀεk   — constant  +  count × gap  +  accumulated noise

Term by term. The first is identical for every sample — a constant offset any readout subtracts. The second is the signal: wᵀδ is the arrow δ converted into a scalar step size Δ along the ruler; every evidence frame pushes the score up by exactly one Δ, so the score's mean is a staircase in the count — the count is linearly present. The third term is the bad news.

the noise term

Independent noises add in variance — the √N fog

Var(Σk wᵀεk) = Σk Var(wᵀεk) = N·σw²  ⇒  std = σw·√N

A drunkard's N steps: forward and backward stumbles partially cancel, so the distance from the start grows like √N, not N. So the staircase keeps its fixed step Δ while the fog around each step widens as √N. (Cross-frame independence is measured, not assumed — that is the ρ card, §2; with ρ > 0 the variance inflates to N(1+(N−1)ρ)·σw², strictly worse.)

the ratio

Count-level discriminability

d′N = d′ / √N

Adjacent counts sit Δ apart in mean under σw√N of noise — gap/width once more. At N = 8: 3.4/√8 ≈ 1.2 — a 96% per-frame decision became a ~73% per-boundary decision, with eight boundaries to get right. This is the whole over-squashing tax in one line: every added frame costs √N even when per-frame supply stays perfectly flat, exactly the measured pattern in §6's N-sweeps (d′ ~2.0 for all N while the model collapses).

the predictor

Under the audited Gaussian shape: the zero-parameter accuracy formula

The best readout of the sum rounds the matched-filter score to the nearest count step (Theorems 1–2, §3). It errs exactly when the accumulated noise crosses half a step — d′/2√N in noise units:

P(exact | interior g) = 2Φ( d′ / 2√N ) − 1  ·  boundary g ∈ {0, N}: Φ( d′ / 2√N )  ·  overall: mix over the count prior

Two measured inputs (d′, N), nothing fitted — this is Theorem 3 of §3 in its working form, and every "predicted" column in §6's parity table is this formula evaluated at a measured d′.

5The E4 adequacy battery

Theorems 1–3 assume "two equal-width Gaussian classes." E4 is the pre-flight check: it asks the data whether that description fits, before any closed-form number is quoted. All four checks run on one object — the held-out projections of messages onto the fitted axis (two histograms, one per class):

pk = wᵀmk (held-out frames)  ·  per class c ∈ {E, N}: standardize  zk = (pk − meanc) / stdc
  1. Skewness ≈ 0 (threshold |skew| ≤ 0.15). Skewness measures lopsidedness: a symmetric bell has skew 0; a distribution with a long tail on one side doesn't. A skewed evidence class means some evidence frames carry systematically weaker signal — the first hint of a mixture. skewc = mean( zk³ )
  2. Excess kurtosis ≈ 0 (threshold ≤ 0.2). Kurtosis measures tail weight relative to a Gaussian (which is defined as 0). Positive = heavy tails: more extreme values than a bell allows. Heavy tails are the fingerprint of a mixture — e.g. crisp + marginal evidence lumped in one class. The law integrates Gaussian tails, so wrong tails = wrong prediction. kurtc = mean( zk⁴ ) − 3
  3. Class std ratio ≈ 1 (accept ~0.85–1.2). Directly tests Theorem 1's equal-covariance premise along the readout axis. If one class is much tighter than the other, the Bayes rule is quadratic and Φ(d′/2) is not the ceiling. ratio = stdE(p) / stdN(p)
  4. d′gap ≈ d′AUC (within a few %). The self-consistency check: the gap/width estimate and the rank-based AUC estimate are two different functionals that coincide exactly for Gaussians and drift apart otherwise. Agreement is positive evidence of Gaussianity, not just absence of a red flag. d′gap = |meanE(p) − meanN(p)| / ( ½(stdE + stdN) )  vs  d′AUC = √2·Φ⁻¹( AUC(p) )
The instrument behind check 4

AUC — threshold-free separability

Area under the ROC curve = the probability that a randomly drawn evidence frame scores higher than a randomly drawn non-evidence frame. 0.5 = chance, 1.0 = perfect. It only uses the ranking of scores, so it needs no Gaussian assumption. For two equal-width Gaussians it maps exactly to d′: AUC = Φ(d′/√2), i.e. d′AUC = √2·Φ⁻¹(AUC). Comparing d′ measured from the gap vs from the AUC is a built-in Gaussianity test — they must agree if the model is right (check 4 above).

What passing buys: the license to use the closed form — to convert a measured d′ into a predicted accuracy with zero fitted parameters, and to call the matched filter a true ceiling. Failing costs only that: d′ remains a valid separability measure, ordering claims survive, but the exact prediction must be caveated.

the localization

The 2×2 and the un-mixers — the tax lives at the query

The message structure (§1), factored one level deeper — same A[c,j], split into scale × shape:

mf = Σj∈f A[c,j]  ·  ŵj = A[c,j] / mf  (Σj∈f ŵj = 1)   — so A[c,j] = mf·ŵj, exactly
msgf = o_proj( Σj∈f A[c,j]·vj ) = mf · o_proj( Σj∈f ŵj vj )   — mass × (within-frame routing · values); identical to §1, o_proj is linear

ŵ is renormalized within the frame, so Σŵjvj is a pure weighted average — all cross-frame competition (the ~1/N dilution) lives in the scalar mf alone. And here is where the query hides inside A:

ŵj = softmaxj∈f( qcᵀ kj / √dh )  ·  qc = WQ·hc  ·  kj = WK·hj  ·  vj = WV·hj

hc is the carrier's residual state at the layer — and in a joint pass hc has already attended over all N frames in every earlier layer. That is the contamination path: the tax can enter through qc (mis-aimed routing) or through hj (degraded frame encoding, moving both k and v) — and the 2×2 separates them. The split makes the channels independently testable:

factorwhat it isverdict on the tax
m_f (mass)how much of the carrier's attention frame f wins inert — renorm null (~8%); excluded from d′ anyway
ŵ_j (routing)WHERE inside the frame the carrier looks — set by its query qc the culprit — see the 2×2
v_j (values)what the frame's tokens carry secondary — degraded but reversible

The 2×2, exactly. For the same sample, two forwards produce two versions of every state: joint (all 8 frames + question) and clean (the frame alone + question, text-padded to 8-frame-equivalent context length, so the ~1.9 long-context term cannot masquerade as the tax). Capture pre-rotary qc, kj, vj from both; then rebuild the message in all four combinations under ONE fixed rotary geometry (the joint forward's positions), with the softmax taken within the frame (mass excluded by construction):

msgf(Q,KV) = o_proj( Σj∈f softmax( q̃c(Q)ᵀ k̃j(KV) / √dh ) · vj(KV) )   Q, KV ∈ {clean, joint} — keys and values travel together (both come from hj)

10The three walls — each problem, concluded; each fix, measured

The model-vs-perfect gap decomposes into three separately measured, separately curable mechanisms. One sentence, three numbers, one fix each.

Ⅰ

Supply — the joint-context tax (§8)

Problem: sharing a forward pass costs ~3.5× per-frame d′ — paid in full at the first companion, carried by an irreducible query-addressing limit (one fixed-width query cannot address N sources).

joint ~2.0 vs multipass 7.2–8.1, flat ∀N · onset 8.08 → 3.37 at k=2 · 2×2: query 6.29 → 1.97 · value repair 93%, query repair −27%

FIX: don't share forwards — per-frame or k≤2 reads; retrieve-then-verify holds 0.79–0.88 to N=128
Ⅱ

Pooling — the √N tax (§4)

Problem: summing N messages before deciding leaves adjacent counts d′/√N apart — even the optimal linear readout of the sum caps at 2Φ(d′/2√N)−1; and at scale the frozen gate's threshold drifts with the 1/N mass dilution.

linear-on-sum ceiling 0.38–0.47 · decode-then-count 0.47–0.59 from the SAME vectors · gate FN 0.09 → 0.99 by N=128 (mass-norm cuts ×10)

FIX: decide per frame BEFORE the sum — sharp gate → tally: 0.09 → 1.00; plus mass-normalization at scale
Ⅲ

Readout — the angle and the clamp (§9)

Problem: the model reads the right token along a nearly orthogonal axis (cos 0.005 to w*, keeping d′ 0.51 of 2.47), its emitted range clamps at scale, and no activation channel is dictatable (the port is a knob, not a number line).

repaired head 0.783 vs frozen 0.133 · law@native 0.17 ≈ model 0.21–0.24 · answers ≈3 ∀N to 128 · every injection fails OOD

FIX: route the count through REAL tokens — tally-as-answer ≈1.0 incl. OOD; the token interface is necessary, not just sufficient
