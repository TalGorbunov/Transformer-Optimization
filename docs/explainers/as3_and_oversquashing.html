AS3 and Over-Squashing

Why a frozen transformer stops counting as the input grows, and how letting it pick and repack its own evidence fixes that in one forward pass.

1. The problem, in one picture

Ask "how many frames show Mary in the kitchen?" over N frames. The answer token gathers evidence from all N frames in one attention step, and attention is a weighted average that must sum to one. Three of the frames are relevant. Watch what happens to their share as the number of frames grows:

N = 83 relevant frames get most of the attention
N = 64the same 3 frames get a quarter
N = 1024they get 6%, the rest is noise

Measured evidence share of the answer token's attention on the frozen 1.5B model (layer 16): .79 → .25 → .06 from N = 8 to 1024. Green is the relevant frames, grey the distractors. Nothing about the relevant frames changed; there are simply more competitors in the average. This is what the GNN literature calls over-squashing: one node (the answer) with too many neighbours, too little signal from each.

2. The same thing as curves

Three numbers describe the read at the answer token: how many frames the attention is spread over (fan-in), what share lands on the relevant frames (evidence mass), and whether the answer is still right. Frozen, fine-tuned at N ≤ 32, and AS3 (also trained at N ≤ 32), all on MMReD text with the 1.5B model:

frozenfine-tuned at N ≤ 32AS3, trained at N ≤ 32

Left: the frozen model's attention spreads over more and more frames (fan-in 15 → 179). Fine-tuning fixes that: fan-in stays at 13–30. AS3 keeps it at about 6. Middle: evidence mass follows the same order. Right: accuracy. Fine-tuning has sharp attention and still collapses at about twice its training length (N = 64). AS3 stays above .9 to N = 128 and .63 at 1024. Shaded band = training range. AS3's middle panel plots evidence influence (share of the answer's gradient on relevant frames), the closest recorded quantity; its fan-in series is the recorded 5-point probe at N = 8–128.

The mechanism in one line. To keep the same evidence share while the number of distractors grows c times, the model must raise its relevant-vs-distractor score gap by log c. A frozen model's gap is fixed by its weights, so its evidence share must fall as N grows.

Why fine-tuning is not enough. It does sharpen the attention (fan-in flat, evidence mass .45 at N = 128). But a model fine-tuned at N ≤ 32 scores .27 at N = 128, while a model fine-tuned at N = 1024 with the same fan-in and mass scores .75. The attention is equally good; what differs is the positions the readout has seen. Fine-tuning fixes the attention, not the decoding at unseen positions. Every fine-tuned model we trained hits its wall at about twice its training cap.

3. It is not only counting

The same measurement on BABILong retrieval (find one fact in a book-length haystack), Qwen2.5-7B frozen, 4k to 128k tokens. The dashed line is the same model reading only the sentences its own attention selected, repacked at compact positions:

full contextselected sentences, repacked

Left: fan-in of the full-context read grows 16 → 78; the repacked read stays at 15–17. Middle: evidence mass .30 → .04 versus .30 → .20. Right: accuracy .92 → .24 versus .94 → .90. Same sentences at their original positions, not repacked, score .54 at 128k: past ~32k tokens, distance matters as much as competition. The pattern repeats on Qwen2.5-3B and Llama-3.1-8B (evidence mass at least halves in 14 of 15 model × task cells). One more result: the textbook "uniform dilution" formula overestimates the evidence mass by 0.4–6.9 nats, because a few sink-like tokens carry most of the competition.

The law. Judge a fix by how the gap between the margin the read needs and the margin the model has grows with N. Fine-tuning shifts the intercept; selecting and repacking makes the slope zero. And whatever does the selecting must itself be length-invariant, or it over-squashes in turn.

4. AS3: select, repack, answer

  1. Encode each frame alone (layers 0–15). Other frames are masked out, every frame gets the same "as-if-first" positions, and the question cannot see the frames yet. Nothing here depends on N.
  2. Select (layer 16). The answer token's attention share on each frame is its relevance score; frames above a fixed threshold (0.5/N) are kept. Both sides of this comparison are N-independent, so the selector cannot over-squash.
  3. Repack and answer (layers 16–27). Dropped frames leave the attention graph; kept frames are renumbered to consecutive positions; the model writes the number itself.
F1F2F3F4F5F6→F1 F4 F6→"3"

Training at N ≤ 32: cross-entropy on the digit through a LoRA, plus a loss that pushes the layer-16 attention toward the model's own zero-shot yes/no judgment of each frame. No gold selection labels, no external model. Deployment: one forward pass, one threshold.

Why "3". AS selected with attention over frames at their native long positions and decayed with length (.44 at N = 128): the selector over-squashed. AS2 made the frame encodings N-independent but still built the selecting query from a diluted read of all frames, and collapsed on transfer (.23 at N = 64). AS3 made both sides N-independent.

Judge recall 1.000 on all three VLMs (86,400 frame judgments on the 7B, zero false keeps). The direct visual question "Is C in this image?" works; the meta-question "is this frame relevant?" scores only .73.

Controls

  • Repack is the payoff: oracle-selected frames masked in place at N = 64 score .25; the same frames deleted and renumbered score 1.00. Positions are labels on the attention edges; rewiring without renumbering breaks the read.
  • Selection is the payoff too: shared positions with nothing dropped scores .15 at N = 32.
  • Training lengths: the answer pass needs only N = 8; the selector needs one distractor-rich length (N = 32 is enough for 1024). AS3 trained at N = 8 only: .68 / .65 / .54 / .28 at N = 16 … 128.

What did not work. The selector cannot be learned from the task loss alone: at initialization the layer-16 attention is uniform over frames, and six controlled attempts at end-to-end learning through differentiable repacking failed, because keeping everything already solves the training lengths. Dense inputs where every frame is relevant leave token-level selection at .51 against a .89 ceiling. Backbones without a retrieval signal (Qwen 0.5B, SmolLM2) gain nothing from any selection method.

6. Where this sits in the literature

line of workwhat it haswhat we add
Over-squashing in GNNs (Alon & Yahav; Topping; Di Giovanni)sensitivity bounded by node degree; rewiring as the curethe transformer instance: fan-in is the degree of the read node, measured on frozen, fine-tuned and repacked models
Softmax dispersion (Veličković; Barbero; Gollapudi; SSMax)attention mass on a target falls with competitors; a mean-margin lawthe law tested on frozen LLMs and falsified by 0.4–6.9 nats; competition vs distance separated with a 1M-context model
Attention-guided selection (SnapKV, GemFilter, InfiniRetri)the model's own attention picks what to keeptheir selector reads the full long input and over-squashes; ours is N-invariant by construction, and we repack positions (75 points)
Retrieval heads (Wu et al.)a few heads copy from context, present in pretrained modelsfor aggregation queries the head is uniform at initialization and is created by our auxiliary loss
Independent context encoding (Parallel Context Windows, APE, register tokens)blocks encoded separately at shared positionsthis is our stage 1; alone it fails (.15) because the read over all blocks is still one average
Co-GNN (Finkelshtein et al.)per-node isolate / listen actions learned against over-squashingthe transformer port, plus what GNNs get for free: action-consistent positions and separate supervision of the action network
Length fine-tuning and position extension (YaRN, LongLoRA)longer usable contextthey sharpen attention but not decoding at unseen positions; the wall moves to ~2× the training cap

Ours in one sentence: the read node of a joint prompt over-squashes in a degree-governed way, fine-tuning changes the intercept of the margin it needs and not the slope, and attention-guided select-and-repack keeps degree, positional coverage and sensitivity constant in N inside one forward pass.

7. Next steps

  • Before the paper (25 Sept): AS3 on the 7B backbone and the current data split; replicate the analysis cells at n ≥ 100 with seeds; the three-panel figure above, redrawn from a single run with all three models on the same probe; move the AS3 scripts into the package with CPU tests.
  • Selector without distillation: use a per-frame yes/no computed behind a fence as the keep signal, so the auxiliary loss goes away and the model still writes its own answer.
  • Dense inputs: token-level selection precision is the open gap.
  • Beyond counting: case analysis over hypotheses and relations between frames; co-occurrence at .81 is the first relational evidence.
  • Composition with reasoning: the same selector and control crossed with direct and free-reasoning outputs in one reasoning backbone; no evidence either way yet.
  • Real data: HERBench showed a per-frame perception ceiling; a real benchmark with reliable per-frame evidence is the main open question.
