Bind, Then Count in One Picture

Counting evidence across many frames with a frozen model, in one forward pass. Text and vision MMReD, September 2026.

1. The task

"How many frames show Mary in the kitchen?" Each frame is one unit of evidence. The answer is the number of matching frames (here 3 of 8). The same question works for text descriptions of frames and for rendered images.

Frame 1: Mary is in the kitchen
Frame 2: John is in the garden
Frame 3: Mary is in the office
Frame 4: Mary is in the kitchen
Frame 5: Bill is in the kitchen
Frame 6: Mary is in the kitchen
Frame 7: John is in the office
Frame 8: Mary is in the garden

2. Before: the normal forward pass, and why it fails

What the model does

All frames sit in one long prompt. The answer token looks at all of them at once through a single attention step, which is a weighted average (softmax). The count has to be read off how much of that average lands on the matching frames.

Exact answers stop at 4 to 8 items. Beyond that the model gives roughly the same number no matter how many frames match. With frames that half-match (same person, other room) it over-counts.

Two things break, both measured causally

The read cannot bind. Attention scores add up features. A frame with Mary in the office gets about 80% of the score of a true match; a frame with Bill in the kitchen only 25%. "Mary" is matched, "Mary in the kitchen" is not, so half-matches leak in.
The share is read as a number badly. Making the matching frames' attention share 4× larger still gives "4 to 6" on Qwen. The map from share to number is compressed. Precision, the softmax normalizer, single heads and a running counter were all ruled out.

3. After: bind first, count second (no training)

What we change

Each frame goes into its own box. Frames cannot see each other (an attention mask, the "fence"), and every box restarts its position count so distance does not matter. Inside each box we append the question and let the frozen model answer yes or no. Then we count the yeses outside the model. One forward pass, zero trained parameters.

F1: Mary in the kitchen
Is Mary in the kitchen here? Answer:
Yes
F2: John in the garden
Is Mary in the kitchen here? Answer:
No
F3: Mary in the office
Is Mary in the kitchen here? Answer:
No
…
→count the yeses = 3

Reading: the fence removes the competition (no other frame in the softmax), the position reset removes distance, the per-box question turns the read into one verdict per frame, and the sum is linear in the count, so length does not matter by construction. In graph terms: cut the cross-frame edges, one virtual node per frame, sum aggregator.

5. Takeaways

One frozen forward pass counts exactly at any length once each frame is judged alone and the verdicts are summed outside the softmax. Text: 1.00 from 8 to 1024 frames. Vision: .97–.99 up to 64 frames, against .20–.40 for the frozen model.

Mask and repack alone do not do it. Letting the model write the number after the fence gives .05–.16. The per-box question is the mechanism, not a detail.

Reasoning models do the same thing serially: one item per step, a written tally on the tape (verified on Qwen3-8B traces). Ours is the parallel form.

Boundary: on real egocentric video (HERBench) the per-frame judgment itself is unreliable (AUC ≈ .78), so no aggregation fix helps there.
